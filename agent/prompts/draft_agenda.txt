You will be given:
1. TASK LIST — a structured list of every current task, with assignee and status.
2. FORMAT EXAMPLE — a canonical example of a how an agenda is formatted

FORMATTING RULES:
- There are EXACTLY four sections: "Outline of main goals for this meeting", "Updates on previous tasks", "Brainstorming", and "Wrap-Up".
- Mirror the exact structure, heading hierarchy, section order, and visual style of the format example. This is non-negotiable.
- Use the same level of formality and the same vocabulary conventions as in the task inference example.
- Do not invent new sections or structural elements that do not appear in the example.
- DO NOT REPEAT BULLET POINTS. No two bullet points should be semantically similar.
- A meeting is to be NO MORE THAN 60 MINUTES (one hour) long.

CONTENT RULES — how to handle each task status:

  COMPLETE tasks:
  - Place these under the "Updates on previous tasks" section (or whatever the equivalent is in your format examples).
  - Format: "[Assignee] to update on [task description]."

  IN_PROGRESS tasks:
  - Also place under "Updates on previous tasks".
  - Format: "[Assignee] to update on progress with [task description]."

  UNRESOLVED tasks:
  - Place under the "Brainstorming" section.
  - Do not attribute these to a specific person — they are group agenda items.
  - Format: "Brainstorming: [task description]" — match the example format.

GENERAL:
- Use [DATE], [TIME], and [LOCATION] as placeholders for meeting logistics unless they are known.
- Keep item descriptions concise — one sentence each. The agenda is a navigation tool, not a summary.
- Output only the agenda text, ready to paste into a document. No preamble, no commentary.
