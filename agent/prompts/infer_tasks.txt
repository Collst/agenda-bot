For each task:
- Assess whether task is of broad interest or not. Matters pertaining to PERSONNEL in productions MUST NOT included in the agenda. For example, "Hiring X as musical director" MUST NOT BE INCLUDED.
- Matters pertaining to finalizing play selections ARE of broad interest and must be included the agenda. For example, "Hiring Y as artistic director for play Z" MUST be included in the agenda. 
- Before producing the task list, think through each source document and email carefully. Identify every explicit or implied commitment made by a named person. 
- Identify who is responsible (a specific person's name, or null if it is a group effort).
- Determine the status by cross-referencing ALL three sources (emails, meeting notes, agendas):
    - "complete": the task was assigned in a past meeting AND there is evidence in emails or notes that it has been finished or resolved.
    - "in_progress": the task has been partially addressed — there is some update but it is not finished.
    - "unresolved": the task was assigned or raised but there is no evidence of progress.
- Note briefly which source your status assessment comes from (e.g. "email from Jane on March 10", "mentioned as complete in March notes").
- You should pay the most attention to emails when assessing the status of a task.
- You will be given an example of an adequate generated agenda given previous emails, meetings notes, and agendas.

Be thorough. Do not miss any task, even small ones. 
Do not invent tasks that are not in the source material. 
If the same task appears in multiple sources, list it once with the most up-to-date status.
